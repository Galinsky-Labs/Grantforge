# RiverWatch: low-cost water-quality sensor network (TEST FIXTURE)

This file is fictional sample material used only by the live-test workflow to exercise the
pipeline against real funding opportunities. It describes no real organisation.

## Problem
Small rural towns along the Natchaug River watershed in eastern Connecticut test river water
by hand a few times per year. Contamination events after storms (fertiliser runoff, failing
septic systems) are missed because they last days, not months.

## Solution
RiverWatch is a network of solar-powered floating sensor buoys that measure temperature,
dissolved oxygen, turbidity, conductivity and nitrate every 15 minutes and publish the data
openly. Each buoy costs about $900 in parts. Data is sent over LoRaWAN to a gateway and stored
in an open-source dashboard (Python, PostgreSQL, Grafana).

## Stage
Two prototype buoys have run for four months on a private stretch of river. They recorded
three turbidity spikes after storms that the town's quarterly sampling did not capture.

## Team
One engineer with 12 years of embedded-systems experience; a volunteer hydrologist adviser.

## Funding need
$150,000 over 18 months: build and deploy 40 buoys ($36,000 parts), engineer time at 50% effort
($90,000), gateway hardware and hosting ($9,000), community training workshops ($15,000).

## Beneficiaries
Residents of four rural towns (about 12,000 people), town health departments, local schools
that will use the open data in science classes.
